# THE FUNDRAISER
## Website Design Pitch Brief
### Concept name: CUT DIFFERENT

Prepared for Izyche, founder of The Fundraiser Show and Fundraisers Web.
Designer and builder: Goodness (Goody).
Status: design proposal for approval. Nothing here is built yet.

***

## 1. The one line pitch

A website that tells your story the way a diamond is made. It starts as a rough stone in the trenches of March 2025, gets cut, faceted and polished with every chapter you survived, and ends as a flawless gem that proves, in public, why projects keep choosing you.

The visitor does not read a portfolio. They watch a diamond get made while your proof of work stacks up around it.

## 2. What you asked for, and where it lives in the design

Your words from the brief, and how each one is answered:

1. "Dark, rich diamond energy". The whole site sits on obsidian black and midnight blue, lit only by diamond light: ice blue, chrome silver, and prism flashes. Gold appears only at money moments, so it feels earned.
2. "The diamonds are important" and "heavy on the diamonds". A living diamond follows the visitor down the entire page and changes form with each chapter. Diamonds are also the bullets, dividers, cursor, loader, card corners and the final call to action.
3. "Keep logo and theme consistent". One logo system, one palette, one type pairing, locked in a brand sheet before a single section is designed. The black and white face logo is the identity. The gold F coin is the money symbol.
4. "Soft, clean, hood, Riley from Boondocks techy, not geek techy". Rounded glass surfaces, calm spacing, confident short copy, a bit of street swagger and humor. No code rain, no neon grids, no robot talk.
5. "A view of who Fundraiser is and what Fundraisers Web agency is". Two dedicated moments: the origin story chapters, and the agency chapter with services.
6. "It's also a proof of work". Every chapter carries receipts: real screenshots, real numbers, real project links, all framed like jewelry pieces in a display case.
7. "All tech and recent projects are important". The recent projects (Clover Boys, Wojak Meter, Ether Archive, Funpump) get the biggest stage. Old calls live in a quiet archive near the bottom.
8. "You don't have to add all my PNLs, just good ones". Only the strongest, verified calls are shown, in a slim carousel, never a wall.

## 3. The big idea: the Cut

A diamond is carbon under pressure. Your journey is the same story: a wallet, a channel, a lot of pressure, and a brand that came out brighter. The site maps your timeline onto the seven stages of making a gem.

1. Rough. March 2025. The Trenches. Web 3 gains, 17 subscribers, sniping and degen calls.
2. Cleave. April to October 2025. The first hard split: from calls to raids and promo work. "Pleased devs, passed projects."
3. Facet. October 2025. The rebrand to THE FUNDRAISER SHOW. New logo, new standard, "we work professionally".
4. Polish. October 2025 to January 2026. The comeback: the drained wallet, the restart, the WISH run, the family hitting 60.
5. Brilliant. 2026. Fundraisers Web at full light: Clover Boys, Wojak Meter, X Spaces, organic growth numbers.
6. Set. June to October 2026. The diamond gets mounted: Funpump ambassadorship, the Ether Archive partnership.
7. Chain. Now. The diamond joins a chain of diamonds. That chain is the contact button.

One scroll. One object. One story.

## 4. What the visitor feels, second by second

1. Seconds 0 to 2. A rough stone sits in black space. A single crack of light cuts across it. It splits into facets and flashes. The page appears. They already know this site is different.
2. Seconds 2 to 10. Huge headline: "Pressure makes diamonds." A slow prism light sweeps across the word. A mono label reads WEB3, DEFI, GROWTH STRATEGY, straight from your brand card.
3. Seconds 10 to 30. They scroll and the stone turns as the story starts. Dates, real screenshots and short lines pull them forward.
4. Seconds 30 to 60. They hit the Clover Boys and Wojak Meter case studies: before and after numbers, live links, the X Space receipt. By now they are not asking if you are legit. They are asking how to book you.

## 5. The signature moments (the parts people will screenshot)

1. The loader that cuts a stone in under two seconds.
2. The hero diamond with real refraction, following the cursor on desktop and device tilt on mobile.
3. The scroll linked diamond that changes through all seven stages.
4. The Riley style mascot (your face logo, redrawn as a sticker) peeking in from page edges with tiny speech bubbles like "you dig?".
5. Proof cards that tilt like jewelry in a display case with a glint sweep on hover.
6. The PNL receipts carousel, framed in ice glass and cropped so the loud green cards stop clashing with the palette.
7. A GM ticker in the footer that mirrors your daily GM posts, because that is part of your voice.
8. The final contact button that builds itself into a diamond chain when the visitor reaches the bottom.

## 6. Pitch talk track (five beats, about five minutes)

Beat 1. Start with his words. Read back his own lines: "rich diamond energy", "heavy on the diamonds", "soft, clean, Riley techy". Show that nothing was ignored.

Beat 2. Show the idea. Put the seven stage diamond strip on screen and say: "Your story is the cut. Rough to brilliant."

Beat 3. Walk the page. Hero, then each chapter, naming the receipt that sits in each one.

Beat 4. Show the polish. Hover states, the loader, the chain button. This is where the "wow" lands.

Beat 5. Close on proof. "This site is not a portfolio. It is a weapon. Every project that lands here sees what you did for the last one."

## 7. What the client gets

1. A one page story site with these sections: Hero, Who is The Fundraiser, six story chapters, Services, Reviews, Archive, Contact.
2. A locked brand sheet: logo rules, palette, type, diamond kit.
3. A mobile version designed first, since most Web3 traffic arrives from phones.
4. Case studies for every recent project, built to be updated as new projects land.
5. Share cards: a custom preview image for Telegram and X so every link looks premium.
6. A simple way to add new projects without touching the design.

## 8. What we need from the client

1. Both logo files in full size, ideally vector or large transparent PNG.
2. Full size originals of every screenshot (current ones are thumbnails).
3. Permission to name Clover Boys, Wojak Meter, Ether Archive and Funpump publicly.
4. Confirmation of every number that will be displayed.
5. His preferred contact: Telegram, X, or a form. Currently @Senor_Izyche on Telegram and @IZYCHE_ on X.
6. Domain name and where it will be hosted.
7. Deadline.

## 9. Decisions only the client can make

1. Is the drained wallet story public? It makes the comeback chapter human and powerful, but it is his call.
2. Is VIC_SONNY an old handle of his? The Phanes cards with the biggest multiples are under that name. If it is not his, they stay out.
3. Does the agency also build websites and utilities, or only grow communities? This decides one service card.
4. Which logo leads: the black and white face or the gold F coin? Recommended: the face leads, the coin supports.
5. Does he want sound? Recommended: off by default, with a small toggle.

## 10. Honesty rules for the whole site

1. Every number shown must come from a screenshot or be confirmed by him.
2. A small, always visible line: "Past results are not a promise. NFA, DYOR."
3. No private messages, prices, or personal data from DMs, ever.
4. Trades done for the community, like RETARD and NAMECOIN, are labeled as community trades, not client work.
